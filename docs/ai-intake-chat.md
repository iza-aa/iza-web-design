# Hyliox AI Intake Chat

## Overview
This is a real conversation with a language model, not a scripted decision tree — that's what makes it feel like talking to someone who's actually listening rather than filling out a form with extra steps. But "real conversation" also means it needs real boundaries: without guardrails, a visitor could just as easily ask it to design a logo, write code, or do anything else a general-purpose model can do, which would confuse this feature with the actual Hyliox AI Studio product it's borrowing its look from. The guardrails are what let this stay a lightweight, free-tier-friendly intake tool while looking like it belongs to the same family as the flagship product.

## Why a Real Chat, Not a Form
A form asks fixed questions in a fixed order regardless of what the client actually needs. A guided conversation can skip questions that are already answered, dig deeper where the client is vague, and handle "actually, let me back up" naturally. That flexibility is the entire reason to use a model here instead of a multi-step form — so don't undercut it by scripting the questions too rigidly in the system prompt. Give the model the list of `extractedFields` it needs to fill and let it decide the order and phrasing.

## Guardrails
The system prompt needs to make three things non-negotiable, regardless of what the visitor types:
1. **Scope lock** — the assistant's only job is gathering enough information to produce the structured brief. It should never attempt to generate a design, write code, or simulate what the finished website might look like, even if asked directly.
2. **Honest framing** — it identifies itself as an AI and states, ideally in its first message, that a human reviews every conversation and replies personally. This is a lead-qualification tool, not a stand-in for you.
3. **Graceful boundaries** — off-topic or abusive input gets a short, friendly redirect, not a lecture. If guardrails trigger repeatedly in one conversation, end it with a plain "let's take this over email instead" fallback rather than looping the same refusal.
4. **Plain text only** — the chat bubble renders the model's prose as raw text with no markdown parser, so `*asterisks*`/`**bold**`/`_underscores_`/`#` headings must never appear in a reply or they show up literally to the client (reported bug, 2026-07-07: `*dark reference*` rendering with the asterisks visible).

Treat the system prompt as a living document — the first version won't get the tone right immediately, and it's worth reading a handful of real transcripts early on to tighten it.

## Borrowing the AI Studio's Look, Not Its Capability
The visual pattern on the real "Imagine it. Iterate it." screen — one large centered prompt box, a row of small utility icons, a model badge, quick-action chips underneath — is worth reusing exactly because it's already the visual language of "this is where you tell Hyliox what you want." Reusing it here means a visitor who's seen the marketing site recognizes the pattern instantly. What doesn't carry over: the **For Human / For Agents** toggle (every visitor here is a prospective client, not an API consumer). The **model picker** does carry over, unlike the original plan — it's genuinely useful here since the free-tier Gemini quota is shared across all visitors, so giving "Auto" a real fallback chain (and letting a visitor pick a specific model) keeps the chat available instead of dying the moment one model's quota is hit.

## Post-Start Layout Shift
The prompt-box card only exists before the first message. The moment the client sends anything, the layout changes shape rather than just growing taller in place: the glass window unmounts (its bubble-thread replacement mounts in its place), a second glass panel — "Your Session," a 5-step progress list (describe → answer questions → pick features & get an estimate → confirm & share contact → sent) — slides in fixed to the left edge of the viewport (desktop only; there's no room for it on mobile), and the textarea + send button relocate into a glass bar fixed to the bottom of the viewport so the conversation itself can scroll like an ordinary chat app. Both the pre-chat window and the session panel carry a single functional mac-style red dot in their chrome bar — clicking it doesn't just delete the element, it plays a "genie" exit (shrink + fly toward the fixed bottom-center dock) before anything actually leaves the DOM; see `hyliox-motion-system.md`'s Close/Genie Exit section for the exact values. Closing the session panel clears the conversation in place (`setMessages([])`, not a hard remount) so the exit animation has something to animate before the pre-chat card reappears; closing the pre-chat card before any message is sent just hides it; a DockNav icon (only present when it's hidden) brings it back and auto-scrolls to it.

## Quick Actions, Repurposed
The three quick-action chips from the reference screen map cleanly onto intake needs once you shift what they trigger:
- **"Recreate Screenshot"** becomes **"Share a reference screenshot"** — the client uploads an image of a site or design they like; it attaches to the brief as-is rather than being fed back into any generation step.
- **"Import from Site"** becomes **"Share a link you like"** — the client pastes a URL; the assistant can ask what specifically they like about it, but never fetches or analyzes the site itself (out of scope, and unnecessary API cost on a free tier).
- **"Explore Effects"** becomes **"Not sure yet — help me explore"** — for clients without a clear picture yet, this shifts the assistant into a slightly more open-ended, brainstorm-style question flow rather than a direct requirements interview.

## Gemini Free-Tier Considerations
Free-tier quotas are shared across all visitors, not per-user, so a couple of things matter more here than they would on a paid plan: keep each turn's context as lean as possible (summarize earlier turns internally rather than replaying the full transcript every request once conversations get long), show a visible "thinking" state rather than a silent hang if a request queues, and have a clear, friendly message ready for the rare case the quota is exhausted for the day ("I'm getting a lot of messages right now — here's a quick form instead" beats a broken chat). None of this needs to be solved perfectly on day one, but the UI should never look like it silently failed.

**Auto model fallback** (`/api/intake`, `src/lib/ai-models.ts`): when the model picker is set to "Auto" (the default), the route walks a fixed chain of free-tier Gemini models — `gemini-3.5-flash` → `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` → `gemini-3.1-pro` → `gemini-2.5-flash` → `gemini-2.5-pro`. Each candidate gets a cheap `generateText` ping (`maxOutputTokens: 1`, `maxRetries: 0`) first; a quota/rate-limit error moves on to the next candidate instead of surfacing an error to the client. The first candidate that pings clean gets the real `streamText` call (also `maxRetries: 0` — the candidate loop *is* the retry strategy, so a single failed attempt should fall through immediately rather than the SDK's default 3x backoff burning more of that model's quota first).

**Multi-account fallback** (2026-07-05): `GEMINI_API_KEY` accepts a comma-separated list of keys — one per Google account, each with its own separate free-tier quota. `/api/intake` builds a client per key and tries every (account × model) combination in order — all models on account 1, then all models on account 2, and so on — so exhausting one account's entire model lineup falls through to the next account rather than surfacing a rate-limited message. Picking a specific model in the dropdown still cycles *accounts* for that one model (the client chose the model deliberately, not the account behind it) rather than skipping fallback entirely. The ping step is skipped for the very last (account, model) combination in the list — there's nowhere left to fall back to at that point, so a single-key/single-model setup (the common case) pays no extra ping latency at all.

Gotcha hit during implementation: the AI SDK wraps a failed call in `RetryError` (with `.errors`/`.lastError`), not a bare `APICallError` — checking `APICallError.isInstance(err)` directly on the caught error always returned `false` and silently defeated the fallback. `isQuotaError` has to unwrap `RetryError` first (recurse into `.errors` and `.lastError`) before checking status code / message shape.

## Conversation & Brief Extraction Flow
1. Opening message: brief self-introduction, states a human reviews the conversation afterward, invites the client to describe their project in their own words first (rather than leading with a question).
2. Guided follow-up: the model fills gaps in `extractedFields` conversationally, using quick actions where the client's response suggests one applies (e.g. mentions "like this site" → offer "Share a link you like"). Every question ends with a `question` block (see Closed-Ended Questions & the QuestionCard); the model is pushed to provide options for almost everything (project type, budget range, timeline, even description/references get example-answer options) and only leave them empty for name/email.
3. Contact info last: name and email are asked as their own question, after every other field, never earlier — see Contact Info Ordering.
4. Wrap-up: once the model has enough signal (including name/email), it writes one short sentence saying the brief is ready — without restating the actual field values, since the `summary` block right after it already renders every field in the "Summary" card (reported bug, 2026-07-07: the model was writing a full sentence repeating project type/budget/timeline that duplicated the card below it) — then emits the `summary` block, immediately followed by a yes/no-shaped `question` block asking for confirmation. If the client picks "something to change," the model asks what (open question), updates the field(s), and re-emits a fresh summary + confirm question — looping until confirmed.
5. On confirmation, the structured `brief` block is generated and emailed per `hyliox-website-system-design.md`'s data flow, and the client sees a short, warm confirmation message.

## Contact Info Ordering
Early drafts let the model ask for name/email whenever it felt natural, which in practice meant the very first or second turn — before the client had said anything about the actual project. That's backwards for a lead-qualification tool: a client who hasn't yet described what they want has no reason to trust the studio with their contact details, and the "Your Session" step panel would sit on "answer a few quick questions" for the entire conversation even after contact info was already given, since nothing marked that milestone. The system prompt now makes it non-negotiable: gather project type, budget, timeline, description, and references first, and ask for name + email as the very last question, immediately before presenting the summary. The prompt also explicitly forbids emitting the `summary` block in the same turn as the name/email question itself (a real failure mode during testing — the model asked for contact info and produced the summary in one turn, with `"..."` placeholders standing in for the answers it hadn't received yet). Summary field values must be the client's actual answer or the exact empty string `""` — never a placeholder token.

`urgency` was dropped from `extractedFields` entirely (2026-07-05) — it read as redundant with `timeline` in the summary card ("Timeline: 2 weeks" next to "Urgency: very urgent" says the same thing twice) and was cut rather than hidden, so it no longer appears in the system prompt's field list, the `summary`/`brief` JSON shape, or `FIELD_KEYS` in `IntakeHero.tsx`.

Because name and email can't be multiple-choice, they don't fit the options-based QuestionCard — but leaving them as a bare options-less question (no card at all) meant a client could submit just a name with no email, or vice versa, with no client-side check. The name/email question now carries `"kind": "contact"` in its `question` block; the client renders a dedicated **ContactCard** (two stacked inputs, name then email) instead of either the option-row card or plain composer, and disables its send button until both fields are non-empty. Submitting sends both values back as one message (`"Nama: X\nEmail: Y"`) so the model gets them together in a single turn, same as it would from typed free text.

## Closed-Ended Questions & the QuestionCard
Every question the model asks ends with a fenced \`\`\`question\`\`\` block: \`{"prompt": "...", "options": [...]}\`. \`prompt\` is a short restatement of the question. The model is instructed to *strongly prefer* filling in 2-4 short options even for softer/open-feeling fields (description, references, "what would you like to change") — since the interface always lets the client type something else anyway, options never cost them flexibility. An empty array \`[]\` is reserved for fields with no sensible finite set at all — name and email are the practical case that comes up.

The client-side extractor strips this block from the displayed prose. What happens next depends on whether `options` is empty:
- **Non-empty options** → the QuestionCard *replaces* the plain composer for that turn (2026-07-05 redesign — previously it stacked above the composer as a row of horizontal chips). Each option renders as a full-width row with a numbered circle badge, rows separated by divider lines (a vertical list, not chips), and a final row — pencil icon, inline text input, send arrow — for typing a custom answer; hitting enter or the arrow there submits it exactly like tapping a row above it. Tapping any row sends its label as a normal user message, same as the existing quick-action shortcuts.
- **Empty options** → no card renders at all; the plain composer stays visible and behaves like any other turn. This was a deliberate simplification (2026-07-05) — an earlier version still popped up a card with just a quiet "answer below" hint for these, which read as an unnecessary popup for something that's just normal free-text chat (e.g. the name/email question).

Only the latest assistant message's question can produce a card, and only while the model isn't mid-stream, so a stale question doesn't linger once the conversation has moved on.

## Language Auto-Match
The EN/ID toggle (top-right) previously only reflected the visitor's browser locale or a manual click — it had no relationship to what language the client actually typed into the chat, so a client conversing entirely in Indonesian could still see English chrome (dl field labels, "Summary" vs "Rangkuman", button aria-labels) if their toggle happened to be on EN. Fixed 2026-07-05: on the client's very first message, a lightweight keyword heuristic (`INDONESIAN_WORDS` in `IntakeHero.tsx`) checks for common Indonesian function words and switches the toggle to `id` if it matches. It only ever switches *to* Indonesian, never forces English, since the default browser-locale detection already handles that case reasonably and a false-positive English switch would be more disruptive mid-conversation. The toggle remains manually overridable at any time afterward. This only syncs the static UI chrome — the model already replies in whatever language the client writes in in regardless (see LANGUAGE rule in the system prompt), independent of this toggle.

Every field label in the Summary/Brief cards is now looked up via `aiIntake.fields.<key>` in the dictionary (`fieldLabel()` in `IntakeHero.tsx`) instead of rendering the raw JSON key (`projectType`, `budgetRange`, …) — the raw-key rendering was the other half of the same "wrong language" bug reported in testing.

## Abuse Lockout
Added 2026-07-05 — the model's own graceful-redirect behavior (Guardrails #3) is good for off-topic or borderline messages, but does nothing to actually discourage someone sending outright profanity/slurs ("woi anjeng" was the reported case) since it just keeps replying warmly forever, and there's real cost: every message, abusive or not, is a paid/quota'd model call. `src/lib/abuse-filter.ts` runs a small, deliberately narrow regex list (unambiguous profanity/slurs only — never mere rudeness or frustration; "ini ribet banget" must never trip it) entirely client-side, before the message ever reaches `/api/intake`. A match never calls the model at all — it immediately locks the whole intake widget.

**Escalating, not forgiving** ("kita bukan sistem kredit" — no free passes): each offense doubles the lockout versus the last one — 1 minute, 2, 4, 8, and so on (`60_000 * 2^(strikes-1)`, `IntakeHero.tsx`'s `triggerBan()`). Strike count and expiry (`{ strikes, until }`) persist to `localStorage` (`hyliox-intake-ban` key) specifically so a page refresh isn't a free bypass. While locked out, the entire intake widget (both the pre-chat card and an in-progress conversation) is replaced by a single `BannedCard` — countdown timer, no composer, no way to send anything until `until` passes — rather than trying to keep the composer half-usable.

Deliberately out of scope for this filter: prompt-injection/jailbreak attempts and general off-topic derailing — those still go through the model's existing graceful-redirect guardrail (Guardrails #3), unchanged. This mechanism is narrowly for the instant, deterministic, no-judgment-call-needed case.

## Auto-Scroll
The chat thread lives in normal document flow (the window scrolls, not a nested scroll container), so nothing kept the viewport pinned to the latest message as replies streamed in — a client would have to scroll down manually to read a reply, or scroll down again mid-stream as it kept growing. A sentinel `<div ref={bottomRef} />` sits after the last bubble; a `useEffect` keyed on `[messages, busy, composerHeight]` calls `bottomRef.current.scrollIntoView({ behavior: "auto", block: "end" })` on every change, so the view jumps to the bottom on each streamed chunk and once more when the reply finishes. Instant (`"auto"`) rather than `"smooth"` scrolling is deliberate — a smooth scroll re-triggered on every streaming chunk fights itself and never settles.

**The fixed composer/QuestionCard bar needs its own clearance, and a parent `padding-bottom` doesn't provide it.** The composer sits `fixed bottom-28`, and its height varies enormously (a plain textarea vs. a QuestionCard with several options vs. a ContactCard) — after a reply that swaps in a taller card, `scrollIntoView` could put the sentinel at the literal bottom of the viewport with the new card's card covering the last message, technically "scrolled to the bottom" but visually wrong. The first fix attempt added `paddingBottom` to the message list's parent container — that doesn't work: `scrollIntoView` aligns *the target element's own edge* to the viewport, and completely ignores box content (like trailing padding) that comes after it in the same parent. The actual fix is CSS `scroll-margin-bottom` on the sentinel itself (an inline style, since the value is dynamic) — that property *is* read by `scrollIntoView` per spec, so the browser leaves exactly that much room below the sentinel. The composer's real rendered height is tracked via `ResizeObserver` (`composerEl`/`composerHeight` state, attached through a callback ref since the card content underneath swaps between `ContactCard`/`QuestionCard`/plain composer while the outer `motion.div` node persists) — `scrollMarginBottom: composerHeight + 112 + 32` (112 = `bottom-28`'s fixed offset, 32 = a small visual gap).

## Session Step Timing
`IntakeStepGuide` marks step `i` as done (✓) only once `current > i`, so the 4-step panel needs a `stepIndex` value of `4` — not `3` — to ever check off the last step ("Sent to {name}"). An earlier version capped out at `3` once the brief was emitted, so the final step stayed on its "active" ring forever, checkmark included never rendering: `stepIndex = latestBrief ? (emailStatus === "sent" ? 4 : 3) : latestSummary ? 2 : messages.length > 2 ? 1 : 0`. While `emailStatus` is still `"sending"` or ends in `"error"`, the panel correctly stays on step 3 (active, not checked) — the brief card's own inline text already explains the send failure, so the step panel isn't lying by omission.

## Fifth Session Step for Feature-Mapping
Added 2026-07-08, once checklist-based feature gathering (see Checklist-Based Feature Gathering above) made the middle of the conversation much longer — several category checklists, the closing check, timeline estimation — all of which used to sit invisibly inside step 2 ("Answer a few quick questions") with no visible progress until the summary appeared. A new step 3, "Pick features & get an estimate" (`stepGuide.step3` in both dictionaries), sits between it and what's now step 4 ("Confirm & share contact," previously step 3) and step 5 ("Sent to {name}," previously step 4) — `IntakeStepGuide`'s `stepKeys` extended to five entries, same generic `.map()` render, no changes needed there beyond the array.

Detecting when to light up the new step needed one addition to `IntakeHero.tsx`'s `stepIndex` calculation: `hasFeatureChecklist` scans every past assistant message (via the same `assistantTexts` array `latestSummary`/`latestBrief` already reduce over, not just the latest turn) for one whose `question` block resolved to `kind: "checklist"`. That mirrors how `latestSummary`/`latestBrief` are found — a full-history scan, not a last-message check — so the step stays lit for the rest of the conversation once the client has seen even one checklist, rather than flickering off the moment a later turn isn't itself a checklist.

## Budget-Range Calibration
The system prompt includes a worked example of a budget-range `question` block so the model's own generated ranges land in a realistic bracket. Most clients coming through this chat are small/local businesses (UMKM), not enterprise budgets, so the example anchors on modest tiers (e.g. "Di bawah Rp1 juta" / "Rp1–3 juta" / "Rp3–7 juta" / "Di atas Rp7 juta") rather than the mid-market ranges ("Rp5–15 juta" etc.) an earlier draft used — the model still generates its own ranges per conversation, this just recalibrates what it anchors to by default, and it can still go bigger if the client signals a larger scale themselves.

## Brief Email as Chat Bubbles
The email `/api/send-brief` sends to the owner used to dump the raw transcript into a `<pre>` block — a wall of "Client: ...", "Assistant: ..." lines that was slow to scan. `IntakeHero.tsx` now sends `transcript` as a structured `{ role, text, images? }[]` array (each message's fenced `question`/`summary`/`brief` blocks stripped first, same extractors used for on-screen rendering, so the owner doesn't see raw JSON) instead of a pre-joined string. The route renders each entry as an actual chat bubble in the HTML email — right-aligned blue for the client, left-aligned gray for the assistant, via inline-styled `<span style="display:inline-block">` (no flexbox, so it survives less-capable email clients). Field values and transcript text are HTML-escaped before interpolation (`escapeHtml()`) — the previous version interpolated both raw, which is an HTML-injection risk in an email your own inbox renders. Field keys in the brief table are humanized (`budgetRange` → "Budget Range") rather than shown as raw camelCase, independent of the visitor-facing `aiIntake.fields.*` dictionary (this email always renders in one fixed style for the owner, not per-visitor locale). `images` (added 2026-07-09, see Image Attachments below) decode from data URL to a `Buffer` and attach via `cid:`, rendered inline inside that same turn's bubble rather than as a separate attachment list at the bottom.

## Retro Canvas / Scrollbar Gutter
Entering the chat view shortens the page enough that it no longer needs a vertical scrollbar, which changes `document.documentElement.clientWidth` — but that transition doesn't fire a `window resize` event, so `AmbientCanvas`'s Three.js `<canvas>` (only resized via a debounced `resize` listener) stayed at its old, narrower pixel width, leaving a plain unpainted strip on the right edge exactly the width of the vanished scrollbar. Fixed by adding `scrollbar-gutter: stable` to `<html>` in `globals.css` — the gutter is now always reserved, so the scrollbar's presence never toggles and the viewport width never changes between the intro and chat views, which removes the trigger for the mismatch entirely (rather than patching `AmbientCanvas` with a `ResizeObserver`).

## Feature-Based Pricing
Added 2026-07-08. Before this, the chat only asked the client's own budget expectation (`budget-range`) and never priced anything itself — the model never broke a project into features, so a client could describe a vague scope in chat, then reveal more features during Iza's manual follow-up call while expecting the price implied in chat to hold. Two new fields, `features` and `price-estimate`, close that gap: once `projectType` is known, the model works through the relevant slice of a fixed rate card (`src/lib/pricing.ts`, injected into the system prompt via `formatRateCardForPrompt()`) in small groups of 2-4 features per question — same conversational pacing as any other field, never a checklist dump. `budget-range` is still asked, but only as an early calibration signal for project scale; the actual quote is the selected base package plus every selected feature, summed from the rate card, never from what the client says their budget is.

Off-rate-card ("custom") feature requests get a rough range, explicitly flagged as needing Iza's confirmation, never a confident exact number. `features` holds the itemized breakdown (each item plus price); `price-estimate` holds the total, always phrased as an estimate. Both render automatically via the existing generic `FieldList`/`fieldLabel` machinery in `IntakeHero.tsx` (`FIELD_KEYS` extended, `aiIntake.fields.features`/`priceEstimate` added to both dictionaries) — no new UI component needed.

**Price objections don't dead-end the conversation.** If the client's reaction at the confirm step is that the price feels too high rather than any field being wrong, the model doesn't negotiate or discount (not its call) — it acknowledges warmly, notes Iza can discuss pricing directly, and proceeds straight to the `brief` block anyway. The alternative — waiting for an explicit "yes" that a put-off client may never give — would mean the contact info they already gave earlier in the conversation (see Contact Info Ordering) never actually reaches Iza by email, since sending is gated on the `brief` block being emitted. Treating a pure price objection as an implicit confirm keeps every conversation a real lead instead of a silent drop-off.

**Language locked to Indonesian/English only** (same date) — previously the model matched whatever language the client wrote in, unbounded. Now it only ever replies in Indonesian or English; if a client writes in some third language, it defaults to Indonesian (the studio's home market) rather than attempting to reply in that language.

**Timeline estimated last, from scope — not asked as an early guess.** `timeline` used to be gathered the same way as any other open field, asked whenever felt natural — often early, before the model knew the actual feature scope, the same problem `budget-range` had before pricing was feature-driven. It's now deliberately sequenced after the entire feature-mapping loop (including its closing check) is done: once the model knows the real scope, it estimates a realistic duration itself from `BASE_DURATIONS`/`formatDurationGuideForPrompt()` in `src/lib/pricing.ts` (a rough per-package-type guide, e.g. "web app/SIM custom: 3-6 minggu," scaled up in the model's own judgment for feature count/complexity — deliberately not a precise per-feature day-count table, since dev time estimates are fuzzier than a fixed price). Only after stating that estimate does it ask the client when they'd actually like it done by. The `timeline` field stores both sides (e.g. "Butuh: 2 minggu (estimasi realistis: 3-4 minggu, dikonfirmasi Iza)") rather than just the client's number — mirroring how `priceEstimate` already surfaces a mismatch instead of silently picking one side.

## Checklist Answer Composition Was Hardcoded Indonesian
Reported 2026-07-08, in an otherwise all-English conversation: the model appeared to randomly switch to Indonesian right after the client submitted a `ChecklistCard`. Root cause wasn't the model at all — `ChecklistCard.submit()` (in `IntakeHero.tsx`) composed the message it sends back as the "client's" answer using hardcoded Indonesian template strings ("Fitur dipilih: ...", "Fitur tambahan: ...", "Tidak ada dari daftar ini.") regardless of the active UI locale. In an English conversation, that meant the client's own message — as the model reads it — suddenly contained Indonesian words, which is exactly the kind of signal the model is told to treat as the client switching languages (see LANGUAGE rule). Fixed by adding `checklistSelectedPrefix`/`checklistExtraPrefix`/`checklistNoneAnswer` to both dictionaries and threading `t` into `ChecklistCard` (same pattern `ContactCard` already used) so the composed message always matches the active locale, not a hardcoded language. The LANGUAGE rule itself was already correct — matching whichever language the client actually types, independent of the EN/ID toggle, symmetric in both directions — this was purely a case of the UI itself putting words in the client's mouth.

## Reliability Fixes: Price-as-Range, Echo-Back, Pre-Summary Checklist
Added 2026-07-08, after real transcripts from the feature-based pricing rework above surfaced three failure modes in the same testing session. Root cause for all three: `INTAKE_SYSTEM_PROMPT` is one large static block sent in full on every turn, and it accumulated a lot of "non-negotiable" rules in one sitting (feature loop, closing check, price objections, language lock, timeline estimation) — the free-tier "Auto" fallback chain in `src/lib/ai-models.ts` deliberately tries the cheapest models first to conserve shared quota, and that ordering is staying as-is (a deliberate call, not an oversight), so fixes need to hold up on a weak model rather than assume a strong one gets picked. A bigger structural fix (server-side conversation-phase tracking, so each turn's system prompt only carries the rules relevant to that phase instead of everything at once) is deliberately deferred — this pass is the cheap, low-risk one first:

1. **Price is a range, never a bare number.** A single precise total (e.g. "Rp4.600.000") reads as a locked quote — once a client has that exact figure in writing, Iza has no real room to revise it upward later without looking like she's going back on it. `priceEstimate` (and any time the total is mentioned in prose, not just the field) is now always a low-high band with an explicit "estimasi awal, bisa disesuaikan Iza" qualifier, e.g. "Rp4.600.000 – Rp5.300.000 (estimasi awal, bisa disesuaikan Iza)." Separately flagged (not fixed here, it's a business call): a few individual rate-card line items — `Login + role user`, `Multi-cabang/multi-outlet` — read as underpriced for their real complexity and are worth the user revisiting directly in `src/lib/pricing.ts`.
2. **Echo-back for ambiguous answers.** A reply like "semua di atas" was previously folded into `features` at face value with no restatement of what that actually meant — risky now that the feature list is the real pricing basis. The model must now restate, by name, exactly which concrete items a broad/ambiguous answer resolves to before moving on.
3. **A mechanical pre-summary checklist**, placed immediately before the `summary`-emission instruction rather than as an early rule diluted by everything after it. This is the direct fix for a reported bug where `timeline` showed up fully populated in the summary despite the client never having been asked — proximity to the action it gates matters more than being "stated somewhere" for a model that may drop rules under load.

## Checklist-Based Feature Gathering
Added 2026-07-08, replacing the multi-round "2-4 features per question, loop until the client says stop" mechanism from earlier the same day. That design was itself the root of two bugs above: the more turns a negotiation takes, the more chances the model has to silently drop a rule (stopping the feature loop early, or emitting `timeline` before ever asking). The fix isn't another prompt rule — it's removing the turns.

A new `"kind": "checklist"` on the `question` block (alongside the existing `"contact"`/`"references"` kinds — same union in `IntakeHero.tsx`'s `Question` type, same `extractQuestion()` parsing) renders as a genuinely different interaction: `ChecklistCard` shows every item in a rate-card category as a checkbox row in one screen, plus a free-text "add another feature" input that appends removable chips rather than submitting immediately (unlike `QuestionCard`'s single-click-to-submit rows). One submit composes everything checked/typed into one message (e.g. "Fitur dipilih: Katalog produk, Testimoni. Fitur tambahan: live chat.") — submitting with nothing selected is valid too ("Tidak ada dari daftar ini."), since a client legitimately needing none of a category's items is a real answer, not a stalled form.

`INTAKE_SYSTEM_PROMPT`'s FEATURE MAPPING & PRICING section now asks one checklist per relevant category (general first, then e-commerce/web-app-SIM as applicable) instead of walking through small groups across many turns — a category's checklist can't be "stopped early," there's nothing left to forget once every item in it is on screen at once. The final yes/no closing check ("Ada fitur tambahan lain yang masih dibutuhkan?") stays as a safety net for anything outside every category's checklist, but it's backstopping a much smaller gap now. The echo-back-for-ambiguous-answers rule (added earlier the same day) narrowed accordingly — checkbox selections are unambiguous by construction, so it now matters mainly for free text typed into a checklist's "add another feature" input or the closing check.

## Contact Method Validation & Compact Buttons
Added 2026-07-08. Two small `ContactCard` fixes reported from a live screenshot: the email/WhatsApp/Instagram method buttons used `flex-1` (stretching to fill the row edge-to-edge) — changed to `inline-flex` in a `flex flex-wrap` row so each button sizes to its own label instead of an artificially wide pill. Second, nothing validated what the client actually typed into the contact-value input — a client could submit "asdf" as their "email." `isValidContact(method, value)` now checks per method (email needs an `@` and a domain, WhatsApp needs a plausible digit-only phone shape, Instagram must start with `@`); the input gets a red border plus an inline hint (`aiIntake.contactInvalid.<method>`) once the client has typed something invalid, and the send button (`ready`) stays disabled until it passes — same pattern as the two-field gate that already existed for name+contact both being non-empty.

## Features Field Dropped Per-Item Prices
Added 2026-07-08. The `features` field in the `summary`/`brief` blocks used to read like a mini invoice — "Company profile (Rp800.000), Katalog produk (Rp300.000), Galeri (Rp150.000)" — duplicating pricing information the `priceEstimate` field already totals up. Simplified to just the feature names ("Company profile, Katalog produk, Galeri"); `priceEstimate` is now the one and only place a price appears anywhere the client sees, matching the earlier CHECKLIST rule that pricing never shows during the gathering phase either. `aiIntake.fields.features` relabeled from "Features & pricing breakdown"/"Rincian fitur & harga" to "Selected features"/"Fitur yang dipilih" to match.

## Image Attachments
Added 2026-07-09 (see `docs/ai-intake-image-upload-plan.md` for the design rationale/tradeoffs — this section is the shipped behavior). The "Share a reference screenshot" quick action used to just send canned text; it now opens a native image picker instead, and a persistent paperclip/image button sits next to the model picker in both the intro and post-start composers, so a client can attach an image at any point, not just via that quick action.

**Client-side (`IntakeHero.tsx`):** the selected file is compressed before it ever leaves the browser — resized to a 1280px max dimension and re-encoded as JPEG at 0.75 quality via an off-screen `<canvas>` (`compressImage()`), regardless of what the client's phone camera actually produced. Non-image files and anything over 15MB pre-compression get an inline error (`aiIntake.imageInvalidType`/`imageTooLarge`) rather than a silent failure. The compressed image shows as a small removable thumbnail chip above the composer before sending, then rides along as a `FileUIPart` (`{ type: "file", mediaType, url: dataUrl, filename }`) passed to `sendMessage({ text, files })` — the AI SDK's `useChat` already supports this natively, no custom multipart/base64 plumbing needed. Capped at 3 images per conversation (`MAX_IMAGES_PER_CONVERSATION`); the attach button just disables past the cap with a tooltip. Sent images render inline in the client's own chat bubble (`renderBubble()`, via `messageImages()` pulling `file`-type parts off a message).

**Hybrid vision, not full vision — the quota-driven tradeoff.** `/api/intake` still resends the entire message history to the model every turn (see Gemini Free-Tier Considerations above) — an unpruned image would get re-uploaded and re-tokenized on every subsequent turn for the rest of the conversation, which is far more expensive than the same treatment for text. `pruneOldImages()` in `route.ts` fixes this: before `convertToModelMessages()`, every message *except the latest one* has its `file` parts swapped for a short text stand-in ("[client shared an image earlier in this conversation — see your own prior reply for what it showed]"). The model genuinely sees and reacts to an image exactly once — the turn it's attached in — and is instructed (IMAGE ATTACHMENTS section, `intake-prompt.ts`) to fold that reaction into the `references` field immediately, the same way a pasted link or gallery pick already works. Later turns lean on that stored text description rather than "looking again." This was a deliberate choice over full vision (re-sent every turn, best UX/worst quota cost) and over email-only/AI-blind (cheapest, but breaks the "real conversation, not a form" premise this whole feature is built on) — see the plan doc's Decision section for the full comparison.

**The original image reaches Iza's inbox too, not just the model's paraphrase.** `/api/intake`'s server-side pruning only affects what's replayed to the *model* — the client's own in-memory `messages` state keeps every original data URL for the life of the tab. When the brief email fires, `IntakeHero.tsx` rides those data URLs along on each `transcript` entry (`images: string[]`, alongside `text`); `/api/send-brief/route.ts` decodes each one (`parseDataUrl()`) and attaches it to the email as an inline `cid:`-referenced image inside that turn's chat bubble (`renderBubble()` in that route, not to be confused with the client-side one in `IntakeHero.tsx`), rather than a separate detached attachment list. So Iza sees the real photo inline, in context, exactly where the client shared it in the transcript — not just the `references` field's text summary.

## Do's and Don'ts
- Do let the model control question order and phrasing — that flexibility is the reason this is a chat and not a form.
- Do disclose upfront that it's AI and that a human follows up personally.
- Do reuse the AI Studio's prompt-box visual pattern exactly — recognizability is the point.
- Do drop the For Human/For Agents toggle — it doesn't apply to an intake tool with no API-consumer audience.
- Do let "Auto" fall back across free-tier Gemini models on quota errors rather than surfacing a broken chat.
- Do ask for name and email last, and only present the summary once they're actually in hand — never in the same turn as the question that asks for them.
- Do emit a `question` block on every question, and prefer real options even for softer/open fields — reserve an empty array for truly unbounded fields like name/email. The QuestionCard shows a vertical option list (not chips) and replaces the composer only when options exist.
- Do let the confirm step be a tappable yes/no `question` block, not just prose asking "sound right?" with no way to answer but typing.
- Do keep the UI chrome's language in sync with what the client actually types (see Language Auto-Match) — don't rely solely on the manual EN/ID toggle or render raw JSON field keys.
- Do keep budget-range examples calibrated to UMKM/small-business scale, not mid-market/enterprise numbers.
- Do escape user-controlled text before interpolating it into the brief email's HTML.
- Don't show a options-less question in a popup card — if there's nothing to tap, it's just a normal chat turn.
- Don't ask about or display a separate "urgency" field — timeline already covers it.
- Do gate the name/email ContactCard's submit on both fields being filled — don't let a client send just one.
- Do auto-scroll the thread to the latest message on every update (streaming chunk or finish) — don't make a client scroll manually to read a reply that's already there.
- Don't let the assistant attempt design or code generation under any framing, even a playful or hypothetical one — that's the one line guardrails must hold.
- Don't replay the full raw transcript on every turn once conversations run long; summarize internally to protect the free-tier quota.
- Don't let a quota or rate-limit hit look like a silent failure — always show the client something, even if it's a graceful fallback to a plain contact form.
- Do keep the abuse filter narrow (unambiguous profanity/slurs only) — a frustrated client typing bluntly must never get locked out.
- Do escalate lockout duration by doubling per repeat offense, and persist it to `localStorage` — a page refresh should never be a free bypass.
- Don't use `padding-bottom` on a scroll-into-view target's parent to reserve clearance below it — `scrollIntoView` ignores content after the target in the DOM; use `scroll-margin-bottom` on the target itself instead.
- Do compress an attached image client-side before it ever leaves the browser — resend size compounds fast once it's replayed across turns.
- Do prune an image out of replayed history once the model has already reacted to it once — never let a raw attachment ride along on every subsequent turn just because it's still in `messages`.
- Don't let the model claim it can't see an attached image — every configured Gemini model supports vision; that's a capability gap the model must never apologize around.
